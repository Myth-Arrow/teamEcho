Two systems, one team

Different jobs, same core. Each one is built to notice something most software ignores.

Running across three machines

HEX

HEX splits itself across a Linux backend, a Windows desktop, and an Android phone, keeping all three in sync over its own network hub. It listens for a wake word, answers out loud through a voice we trained ourselves, and remembers what it's told well enough to bring it up again later. Left alone, it spends its idle time searching, reading, and learning — then quietly files away whatever it found worth keeping.

In active development

E.C.H.O

E.C.H.O grew out of HEX's core, rebuilt to watch a room instead of a chat window. It checks every file that comes in for signs of something hidden, keeps an eye on a 3D printer's telemetry, and senses whether someone's nearby through a motion sensor and a pose model — without ever saving an image. One internal system weighs all of that at once to decide if a person should be interrupted at all.

What un-ordinary looks like

Not a demo, and not a chatbot with a personality bolted on. This is what's actually running.

  • Reads a room without recording it

    A motion sensor gates a single pose estimate at a time — nothing is ever written to disk.

  • Flags what shouldn't be there

    Every incoming file gets hashed and checked for hidden data through entropy analysis, automatically.

  • Decides whether to interrupt you

    One state machine fuses location, motion, screen activity, and even print jobs before deciding if now's a good time.

  • Teaches itself between conversations

    Idle time goes into searching, reading, and quietly filing away whatever's worth remembering later.
